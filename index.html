Companion analysis to The Three FT50s & The ABS 1,583

The JUFO 24,194

Why this page exists

The Finnish Publication Forum (JUFO) classifies publication channels into levels 3 (highest), 2, 1 (basic) and 0. The levels steer how Finnish universities are funded and, in practice, where Finnish researchers try to publish. The panels revise the classification through public comment rounds — such as the current call for comments on changes to level 2.

If you are commenting on a journal, look at the big picture first. This page puts every Scopus-indexed journal on the JUFO list on the same measurement scale — the 25 SciVal indicators used in the FT50 and ABS companion studies — so a comment can be grounded in evidence rather than familiarity:

  • Where does the journal actually sit inside its current level? (§2, §3, and the explorer in §11 — every journal has a percentile profile.)
  • Is the case you are making visible in any indicator — citations, usage, policy or patent reach — or is it purely reputational? (§1, §7, §8)
  • How do international ratings treat the same journal? JUFO, ABS/AJG and the FT50 agree far less than most researchers assume. (§5)

What we found

1 · Impact tracks the JUFO level — unevenly

Each bar is the median journal of a level, scaled so level 3 reads 1.0. If the levels measured one thing called “quality”, all four channels would fall in step. They do not: citation impact and policy reach drop steeply level by level, usage (views) barely moves, and patent reach is essentially flat — the same asymmetry the ABS study found across the AJG ratings.

2 · The levels overlap — a lot

Each strip shows where a level’s journals sit in the whole JUFO population, ranked by field-weighted citation impact (FWCI). The thick bar is the middle half of the level (25th–75th percentile), the whisker the 5th–95th, the notch the median. The levels are real strata — but wide, heavily overlapping ones: a level is a panel judgement about a distribution, not a property of every journal in it.

A sharper separator is journal standing: the share of a journal’s output published while the journal ranked in the top decile of its field by CiteScore. By this measure the levels separate cleanly at the top — and levels 1 and 0 are indistinguishable.

3 · The edge cases — journals that don’t fit their level

These are the journals worth a comment. The rule is deliberately simple: rank the 23,749 journals with the required indicators by the broad-impact index — the mean of a journal’s percentile on citation impact (FWCI), field-normalised policy impact, and patent citations per 1,000 papers, the same three channels the FT50 study used. A journal is a promotion case when its index sits above the median of the level above. A demotion case must clear two bars: index and field-weighted citation percentile both below the medians of the level below — the second condition stops fields with no patent or policy uptake (mathematics, large parts of the humanities) from being flagged mechanically. Journals with at least 100 lifetime papers; the lists show the twenty strongest cases per boundary, the counts the full scale.

      6 · The Finnish footprint

      Where do Finnish researchers actually publish inside this system? Every Finland-affiliated paper in the 23,588 OpenAlex-matched JUFO journals, 2010–2026 — papers in all, attributed through the 840 Finnish institutions registered in OpenAlex. Finland is over-represented at the top: its share of world output is highest in level-3 journals and falls with every level.

      And who publishes it: each bar splits an institution’s JUFO-journal output by level. University hospitals and research institutes ride the medical level-2/3 journals; the multidisciplinary universities carry the biggest level-1 volumes.

      Whole counting: a paper with authors at two Finnish institutions counts once for each — summed institution bars reach ~1.7× the 311,966 distinct papers, so do not add bars together. Papers and citations are OpenAlex measures (citations run higher than Scopus/SciVal); the share denominators are SciVal Scholarly Output, so treat shares as close approximations. The explorer below has a per-journal FI papers column.

      7 · Better fits, worse fits — the spectrum inside each level

      Every level has a top end and a bottom end. To see them on one scale, each journal gets a broad-impact index: the mean of its percentile on citation impact (FWCI), field-normalised policy impact, and patent citations per paper — the FT50 study’s three channels. Within its level, every journal is then a quartile: Q1 (top 25%) to Q4 (bottom 25%). The question for Finnish science: does Finnish volume flow to the top of each level, or to the easy end?

      The same question journal by journal: how heavily Finland uses a venue against how its Finnish papers perform. The impact measure is year-normalised within the Finnish corpus — 1.0 means the papers are cited like the average Finland-affiliated JUFO paper of the same age; the index on the x-axis says how strong the journal itself is. Neither measure is field-normalised against its own discipline: a mathematics or humanities journal sits low on both axes by field alone, so read positions within a field, not across fields. With that lens, top-right is where volume pays off, and a big dot low on both axes is worth a second look.

      Method & caveats

      The JUFO classification covers journals, series, conferences and book publishers. This analysis covers the journals that have a Scopus record and therefore SciVal metrics — 24,194 titles tagged by level from the julkaisufoorumi.fi list. Conferences, book publishers and non-indexed channels are outside it. Publication years 2010–2026.

      Read the levels as strata, not as ground truth. A JUFO level is a panel judgement; this page uses it to slice the population. §2 is the reason to be careful: the distributions inside neighbouring levels overlap heavily, so the level alone says little about an individual journal.

      Field normalisation matters — and is incomplete here. Raw counts (citations, views, policy documents) are not comparable across fields. FWCI and the field-normalised policy indicator carry most comparisons, but the broad-impact index’s patent channel is a raw cross-field rate, and the §7 Finnish impact measure is normalised by year only — both sink low-patent, low-citation fields by construction. JUFO panels are field-specific for exactly this reason; so should any reading of §3 and §7 be. Data through mid-2026; the 2026 publication year is partial.

      Comparability with the FT50/ABS pages. The JUFO export uses the self-citation-excluded citation variants and a 2010–2026 window; the FT50/ABS pages use including-self-citation variants over 1996–2026. Cross-page comparisons should stay qualitative. The ABS crosswalk matches 1,520 of 1,583 Scopus-indexed AJG journals by name; the FT50 crosswalk matches all 53.
